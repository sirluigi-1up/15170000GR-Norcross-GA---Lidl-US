## BOTTOM LINE (read first)

1. **The GC's position is only partly supported, and the strongest support is Georgia law, not federal law.**
   - **Georgia (supports the GC):** The state duty to notify belongs to the contractor doing the work. The Act says "the contractor" must notify at least 7 days before it starts, and *"No contractor shall engage in a project prior to notifying the Director"* (Rule 391-3-14-.02(2)).
   - **Georgia EPD practice (supports the GC):** EPD requires 10 working days. The Georgia-licensed abatement contractor's agent completes the abatement notification, and each firm files it through GEOS under its own company ("facility") account.
   - **Conclusion on Georgia:** Contractor A's notification is A's filing. Contractor B, as a different licensed contractor, has its own duty to notify before it starts any regulated (friable/RACM) project.
2. **No source says "notifications are not transferable."** I searched EPD's notification page, forms page, FAQs, technical guidance, the GEOS guides and the "Just the Basic Facts" sheet. None of them says notifications are non-transferable, or that a contractor change requires a new notice with a new 10-working-day wait. **Do not quote "not transferable" as EPD language.** Present it as a reading of the statute and rule (the duty sits with the contractor that will "engage in" the project), or get it confirmed in writing by EPD.
3. **Federal NESHAP does NOT clearly support the GC's position.**
   - 40 CFR 61.145 puts the duty on the "owner or operator." That term is broad enough to cover the GC, the owner, and either abatement contractor.
   - The rule expressly allows "revised" notices (§61.145(b)(4)(i)) and updating the notice "as necessary" (§61.145(b)(2)).
   - The only federal change that restarts the 10-working-day clock is an **earlier** start date (§61.145(b)(3)(iv)(B)).
   - The federal text says nothing specific about changing contractors. Several non-Georgia agencies treat a contractor change as a simple **revision**.
   - **Risk:** Under federal law alone, a revised notice naming B, with the same or a later start date, filed before A's original start date, arguably needs no new 10-day wait.
   - **Exception:** If A's original start date passed and no revision was filed by that date, a later start was not properly noticed under §61.145(b)(3)(iv)(A). A fresh 10-working-day notice is then the conservative route.
4. **Non-friable mastic is a major caveat that may cut against the delay claim.**
   - Georgia's licensing and notification statute and rule cover only **friable** ACM.
   - EPD's May 2025 "Just the Basic Facts" sheet treats removal of non-friable ACM as a **"Courtesy" notification (No Fee)**. Unlike the abatement and demolition notices, it does **not** carry the "10 working days before start" marker.
   - Federally, Category I non-friable floor mastic and asphalt roofing mastic are not RACM unless they become friable, or are sanded, ground, cut or abraded (§61.141).
   - **Result:** If B removes the mastics by non-mechanical methods (chemical, dry ice, infrared), there may be **no mandatory 10-working-day wait for B's abatement at all**, only a courtesy notification. The mandatory 10-day wait would apply only if B's method makes the material friable (for example mechanical chipping or grinding) or the quantities trigger RACM.
   - The sub's description of a "courtesy notification" for non-friable mastic **is consistent with EPD guidance**.
5. **The demolition notice is a separate filing.**
   - It is required federally even with no asbestos (§61.145(a)(2)) and by Georgia EPD for every demolition.
   - It is due 10 working days before demolition and is filed by the contractor performing the demolition.
   - It does not depend on which abatement contractor is used. It needs revising only if its dates or contents change. Any delay argument should rest on demolition having to wait for the abatement to finish, not on the abatement notice alone.
6. **Recommended evidence for the claim letter:** Get a written statement from Georgia EPD's Lead-Based Paint & Asbestos Program confirming that B must file its own notification and wait 10 working days.
   - Contacts: Duty Officer 404-363-7026; leadpaint.asbestos@dnr.ga.gov.
   - Give EPD the scope and removal method: friable/mechanical versus non-friable/chemical.
   - That statement would be the strongest authority available. I found none published.

**Points against the GC position:**
- The federal duty is on the "owner or operator," which can include the GC.
- The federal scheme contemplates **revised** notices, and the required contents include the contractor's identity. That implies a contractor change is handled by a revised notice, not necessarily a new original notice.
- Only an earlier start date expressly triggers a new 10-working-day period.

**On the sub's "courtesy notification" statement:**
- It is **accurate** for non-friable mastic removed by non-friable methods.
- It is **not accurate** if removal will be mechanical (grinding or chipping) or otherwise makes the material friable. In that case it is a licensed abatement project with a fee and 10 working days.
- In both cases, **demolition itself still needs a Demolition Project Notification**, 10 working days ahead, under both EPD guidance and 40 CFR 61.145(a)(2) and (b)(3)(i).

## Suggested framing for the claim letter (accuracy-first)

- **Safe to state:**
  - Georgia law places the duty to notify on the licensed contractor that will perform the project (O.C.G.A. § 12-12-10; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 391-3-14-.02(2)).
  - EPD requires notifications to be submitted through GEOS by the licensed abatement contractor's agent, 10 working days before work.
  - EPD grants no waivers of the notice period.
  - B cannot begin a regulated abatement until a notification covering B's work has been on file with EPD for 10 working days.
  - Demolition requires its own 10-working-day notification regardless of asbestos.
- **Avoid stating as fact:**
  - That EPD says notifications are "not transferable."
  - That federal NESHAP requires a new notice and a new 10-day wait for a contractor change. Federal law frames this as a revision, and only an earlier start date restarts the 10 days.
- **Address up front:** whether B's mastic removal is friable or mechanical (a regulated project with a 10-day wait) or non-friable or chemical (courtesy notification only, arguably no mandatory wait). This drives whether the delay is legally compelled.
- **Strengthen with:**
  - A written EPD confirmation.
  - The dates of A's original notice: its listed start date, and whether a revision was filed by that date (40 CFR 61.145(b)(3)(iv)(A)).
  - The date B's notice was filed, and the resulting earliest lawful start date.
